Things that are good to know
Fungoida is fundamentally a subsurface organism, meaning what you see on the surface is only the reproductive “tip” of a much larger underground structure.
Unlike Frutexa, it is terrain-driven rather than pure atmosphere-driven — elevation is a major hidden requirement.
The DSS “blue zones” can be misleading because spawn depends heavily on micro-terrain (cliffs, ridges, plateaus) rather than general biome coverage.
CO₂-based species introduce a strict thermal band (180–195K), making them one of the more constrained exobiology groups.
Although classified as flora, Fungoida behaves closer to a fungal colony network system, similar to distributed biological infrastructure.
It often overlaps spatially with Frutexa, but they occupy different ecological layers (Frutexa = surface shrubs, Fungoida = subsurface fungal network).
Perceived rarity is frequently caused not by absence, but by spawn geometry (tiny valid terrain pockets inside large invalid regions).
This genus is one of the most terrain-sensitive detection systems in Elite exobiology, making it heavily dependent on exploration technique rather than raw presence.
