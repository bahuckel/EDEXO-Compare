What this app does with data

ED Exo Compare is built to read Elite Dangerous journal files and other local game-related data that the game writes on your computer. Core functionality is designed to process that information on your device (for example, to merge scans, show candidate species, and surface route or payout estimates).

The app also stores working data under its own folders (for example species tables, cached prices, catalogs, and merger or UI state). That data stays with your install unless you copy or back it up yourself.

Network and third parties

The app contacts two third parties, and only these two. Both are named here rather than covered by a general clause, because you should be able to read what leaves your machine without running it.

EDSM (Elite Dangerous Star Map) — edsm.net

EDSM is a community database of star systems. The app asks it about systems, using these public endpoints: api-system-v1/bodies and api-v1/systems (system and body data), and api-status-v1/elite-server (game server status). What is sent is a system name, and nothing else about you or your game.

There are two ways this happens:

  • When you ask. Loading a system’s map or searching for a system by name sends that one request, when you click.
  • Automatically on jump, if you switch on EDSM auto-fetch in Options. It is off by default, and it stays off until you enter your own EDSM API key. When it is on, the name of each system you enter is sent to EDSM as you arrive, at most once per system, so the app can show you what is there before you finish travelling.

Auto-fetch requires you to be a registered EDSM user and to supply your own API key from edsm.net/en/settings/api. Your EDSM commander name and key are sent with those automatic requests, so the traffic is attributable to your account rather than to an anonymous client. The key is stored on your own machine, in its own file next to your app settings; it is never included in the settings file, never sent anywhere except edsm.net over HTTPS, and you can delete it from Options at any time, which also switches auto-fetch off. Your use of EDSM is subject to EDSM’s own privacy policy.

ipify — api.ipify.org

When you click the eye icon beside Public internet in the launcher’s network settings, the app asks ipify what your public IP address is, so it can show you the address a phone or tablet outside your home network would use. This happens only on that click — never automatically. The address is stored on your device so the launcher can show it again, and the Forget button in the same panel deletes it.

Nothing else

There are no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting and no update checks. Your journal files, your scans, your discoveries and your commander name are processed on your machine and are not uploaded anywhere. This policy will be updated if that ever changes.
